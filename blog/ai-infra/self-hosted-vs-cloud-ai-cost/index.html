Read the labor line twice: it is where self-hosting loses small deployments and wins disciplined ones. The automated stack from this cluster (gateway quotas, journaling, heartbeat monitoring, IaC rebuilds) holds ops near two hours weekly; unautomated stacks double it and invert the comparison at every volume -- automation is not a virtue here, it is the cost structure, exactly as the exit guide's labor analysis found for general infrastructure.

The unpriced columns, named anyway

  • Privacy and data gravity: prompts never leaving your estate has compliance and IP value no list price captures; the privacy piece sizes what leaves by default.
  • Pricing immunity: vendor price and model-deprecation shocks become news, not decisions; budget planning loses a volatility column.
  • Model freedom: fine-tunes, quantization experiments, and mixture routing per workload -- per-slot model choice is a quality lever cloud tiers rent back to you as plan upgrades.
  • Latency locality: on-LAN inference removes WAN round-trips from interactive loops; at drafting cadences, 40 ms saved per token stream is felt, not measured.

The hybrid answer most estates actually land on

For readers building their first sheet, three shortcuts keep it honest: price cloud at the p95 month, not the average (bursts are the bill); value ops labor at your real replacement cost, never zero (zero is the number that makes sheets lie); and amortize hardware over three years with a residual note, then re-run at two and four years as sensitivity rows. Three sensitivity rows convert a verdict into a range, and ranges survive contact with next quarter's pricing email -- which is the entire purpose of the exercise: not to declare a winner forever, but to own the arithmetic well enough that forever is not required, and every renewal conversation starts from your ledger instead of their brochure. Keep every yearly sheet in the config repository beside the gateway configs, dated and signed by whoever ran the numbers; a cost history you own is the only inflation index that applies to your actual workload, and it compounds in credibility exactly as the proofs compound in trust.

What volume should trigger the first GPU purchase?

When CPU-only latency stops serving your interactive slots (per the CPU piece's concurrency physics) or when monthly cloud spend exceeds the used-GPU price divided by 24 -- whichever arrives first. Both are gateway-log queries, not forecasts: buy after the measurement shows the shape of your traffic, and let the journal entry that justified the purchase become the first line of the asset's design note.
