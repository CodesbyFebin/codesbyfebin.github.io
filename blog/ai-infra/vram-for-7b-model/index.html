How Much VRAM Do I Need for a 7B Model at Q4, Q8, and FP16?

Exact VRAM math for 7B models at Q4, Q8 and FP16 including KV-cache headroom, plus the settings that fit a 7B model into 6 GB and 8 GB cards.

The three-term arithmetic

VRAM demand is the sum of three terms. Weights: parameters x bytes-per-weight (FP16 = 2, Q8 = 1, Q4 = 0.5, plus roughly 3-5% overhead for quantization scales). KV cache: 2 x layers x kv-heads x head-dim x context x bytes-per-element, doubled because key and value are separate tensors. Overhead: CUDA/graph arenas, allocator fragmentation, and the runtime's own footprint -- budget 0.5-1.5 GB and treat anything less as luck. For a Llama-shaped 7B (32 layers, 32 kv-heads pre-GQA, head-dim 128) at FP16 with 4k context, the KV term alone is about 2 GB; with GQA (8 kv-heads) it drops to about 0.5 GB, which is why modern 7B models fit 4-bit plus 8k context inside 6 GB cards.

Read the last row carefully: context length is the silent budget-killer. KV cache grows linearly with context and with batch size, so a 4-bit model that idles at 5 GB can exceed 9 GB under a long document or a few concurrent users. If you serve rather than chat, cap context and batch explicitly -- the cost model in the ai-infrastructure reference stack treats "VRAM per concurrent session" as a first-class capacity unit for exactly this reason.

When a community post claims "7B fits in 4 GB", compare their KV line and context to yours before believing the headline -- most such claims are 2k context, batch 1, and no headroom.

Making 7B fit a 6 GB or 8 GB card

  • Choose Q4_K_M or IQ4_XS -- the K-quants keep attention weights sharper than naive 4-bit; quality loss on coding and math benchmarks is typically 1-2%, far less than the loss from dropping to 3-bit.
  • Cap context to what you use (--ctx-size 8192), and enable flash attention so the KV term stays resident-efficient.
  • Offload nothing if it fits: partial GPU offload (some layers on CPU) saves VRAM but can halve throughput; it is a fallback, not a tuning strategy.
  • One model per card: two "small" models sharing 8 GB fragment the allocator and OOM at the worst moment; use a gateway with queueing instead, as in the XFree local-assistant configuration.

The privacy angle deserves one sentence because it drives most of these deployments: a 7B at Q4 on a six-year-old GPU produces genuinely useful assistant, summarization, and classification capacity with zero tokens leaving your building -- the threat-model argument is developed in the cluster index on AI Infrastructure & MCP, and the services page covers assisted setups.

Does quantization change the VRAM math for CPU-only boxes?

The arithmetic is identical, but the scarce resource becomes RAM bandwidth, not capacity: a Q4 7B needs about 5 GB of RAM yet generates tokens at whatever your memory bus allows (typically 4-9 tok/s on DDR4). Capacity questions and speed questions have different answers on CPU -- see the cluster piece on CPU inference when it ships.

Why does my measured VRAM exceed the sum of weights and KV?

Allocator arenas and compute graphs reserve contiguous blocks; CUDA context itself takes 300-500 MB before your model loads. Measure the empty-runtime baseline first (load nothing, read nvidia-smi), then subtract -- the residual is your true overhead term, and it is mostly fixed per process.

Is FP16 ever worth it for a 7B model locally?

For quality-sensitive evaluation runs and as a baseline when benchmarking quantization loss, yes -- that is exactly what it is for. For daily assistant use, Q8 is visually indistinguishable in most tasks at half the VRAM, and Q4_K_M is the pragmatic daily driver; keep FP16 as the ruler, not the tool.
