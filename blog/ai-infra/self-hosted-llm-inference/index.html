The gateway: quotas, audit, and one API shape

Hardening the serving box

An inference box holds your organization's most sensitive traffic in RAM -- prompts, documents, code -- so harden it like the asset it is. Egress-deny the serving process by default (a model needs no outbound calls; updates ship as signed artifacts, not runtime phone-home); isolate tenants with separate keys, quotas, and where regulation demands, separate model replicas, because VRAM spent on containment is cheaper than the alternative's audit findings; keep prompt-body logging off by default with opt-in windows under a retention clock, since the journal should prove behavior without becoming the data leak you built the stack to avoid. Borrow one more control from the proof cluster: hash-pin your weights. The gateway refuses to route to any replica whose loaded artifact hash mismatches config, so model swaps -- accidental downloads, poisoned mirrors, a well-meaning colleague's "better" checkpoint -- surface at startup instead of as a quarter of subtly different answers.

Pinning also makes model identity an operational fact rather than a memory: "which model answered" joins "who asked" and "what was permitted" in the audit line, and incident reviews stop beginning with archaeology. In a stack built for sovereignty, that triad is the whole point -- control over the wire, control over the weights, and control over the record of both.

Observability: five signals, no more

  • Queue depth and wait p99: the leading indicator of under-provisioning, minutes before latency moves.
  • KV-cache utilization: above 85% sustained, preemption storms follow; alert at 80.
  • Tokens/s per user and per watt: the efficiency pair that makes cost conversations factual.
  • Context-length histogram: workloads drift upward silently; the histogram catches the month your 4k default became 12k reality.
  • Cache hit rate: below 20% means your users' traffic is less repetitive than assumed -- or your keys are wrong.
When should I just use a managed API instead?

Bursty demand (a quarterly job), frontier-model-only quality needs, or below the break-even token volume the cost comparison computes for your hardware class. Self-hosting wins on steady state, privacy, and pricing immunity; managed wins on elasticity and the newest weights. The gateway makes the question per-workload rather than religious -- route the burst out, keep the steady in, and let the audit log settle arguments with numbers.
