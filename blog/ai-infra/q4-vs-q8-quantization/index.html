Why the loss lands where it lands

Quantization error is not uniform noise: importance-aware schemes protect salient channels, so common patterns survive while rare, precise weight configurations -- exactly what exact-recall and long-chain composition depend on -- absorb the rounding. The behavioral signature follows: fluency and gist hold (distribution-typical outputs), precision tails suffer (distribution-atypical outputs). This is also why vibe checks mislead: ten casual prompts feel identical across quants while your twentieth, hardest workflow degrades; harnesses exist because humans sample the easy tail by instinct. The quantization explainer carries the format-level mechanics; this page prices the consequences.

Speed and memory: what you buy with the loss

The twenty-minute decision protocol

  1. Pull 50 real requests from your gateway journal or workflow logs (the gateway piece makes this a query, not a project).
  2. Score Q8 as baseline with an automatic metric plus ten human-rubric samples.
  3. Score Q4_K_M identically; compute per-family deltas against the table above as sanity, not substitute.
  4. Decide per deployment slot: daily-driver chat and drafting tolerate Q4; recall- or code-critical slots get Q8 if memory allows; pin whichever artifact hash the decision produced.

Twenty minutes, two numbers, one pinned hash -- and the decision re-runs itself whenever models or runtimes upgrade, because the request corpus lives in-repo beside the scoring script. Teams that skip the protocol do not skip the decision; they make it by folklore, and folklore's quantization opinions are usually somebody's GPU vendor's marketing wearing a benchmark's costume.
