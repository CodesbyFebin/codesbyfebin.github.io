Does a Local LLM Hallucinate More Than a Cloud One?

Do local LLMs hallucinate more than cloud ones? What changes with size and quantization, how to measure hallucination rate on your corpus, and mitigation stacks.

The variables that actually move hallucination

  • Scale and training mix: the dominant terms; frontier cloud models lead here, and no deployment choice changes it.
  • Context discipline: over-long or contradictory context raises confabulation in every model class; retrieval quality (the RAG guide's eval loop) moves local hallucination more than any server decision.
  • Sampling settings: temperature and top-p drifts affect local and cloud equally; pinned configs in your gateway make this a controlled variable instead of folklore.

Measuring your own rate, in one evening

Controls that beat deployment-location anxiety

  1. Citation enforcement on fact-mode prompts, with post-processing that flags uncited factual claims (the RAG guide's trust architecture).
  2. Refusal training by prompt contract: explicit "say you cannot verify" instructions measurably convert hallucinations into refusals at small quality cost -- refusals are recoverable errors; confabulations are not.
  3. Verification gates for consequential outputs: code goes to compilers and tests, numbers to recomputation, citations to resolver lookups -- machines checking machines, the portfolio's standing pattern from proofs to patches.
  4. Tier routing: recall-heavy and citation-bearing workloads to Q8-or-cloud slots; drafting and transformation to Q4 local; the gateway's alias table makes tiering invisible to clients.

The honest summary for decision-makers

Does longer context increase local hallucination more than cloud?

Context-length sensitivity scales with model class, not hosting: small models degrade faster past their trained context sweet spot, and KV quantization (a local memory-saving habit) adds a second degradation source at long contexts. Keep long-context slots at Q8 KV or above, and chunk-and-retrieve instead of pasting corpora -- the same advice the RAG and VRAM guides give from their own angles, converging because the physics is shared.

Can self-consistency sampling fix local hallucination cheaply?

Partially and measurably: majority-vote over 5 samples cuts trap-class hallucination roughly in half at 5x compute -- affordable on CPU batches overnight, painful interactively. Use it for offline artifacts (summaries, extractions into records) where latency is free, and spend interactive budget on retrieval and citations instead; sampling tricks are batch economics, not conversation economics.

Should user-facing UIs disclose local model use?

Yes, plainly: a one-line model-and-tier indicator per response (name, quant, routing exception if any) matches the citation philosophy and costs nothing. Disclosure converts user calibration from guesswork into habit -- calibrated users report fewer trust incidents and file better feedback, and better feedback is the eval set's most renewable source, closing the loop between honesty and quality in a way no benchmark purchase can.
