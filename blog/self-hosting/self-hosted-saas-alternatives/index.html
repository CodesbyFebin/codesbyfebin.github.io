Self-Hosted Alternatives to SaaS Tools: 31 Swaps That Stick

Why some swaps stick and others don't

The list, with honest swap costs

  • Notes: Obsidian-over-Syncthing (files stay yours, zero server) or Nextcloud Notes for shared; cost: sync-conflict discipline.
  • Mail: Stalwart or Maddy plus SOGo; cost: deliverability work (PTR, SPF, DKIM, warm-up) -- the one swap where the server is easy and the reputation is the project.
  • Photos: Immich; cost: face-recognition CPU budget and mobile auto-upload tuning; benefit: Google-Takeout-sized imports land in an hour.
  • Passwords: Vaultwarden per the dedicated guide; cost: near zero; benefit: immediate and total.
  • Chat: Matrix/Element; cost: federation optional but tempting, bridge maintenance if you keep old networks.
  • Analytics: Plausible/Umami; cost: losing ad-network integrations you never used anyway.
  • Office: Collabora/OnlyOffice; cost: macro-heavy spreadsheets remain the honest exception -- keep one cloud seat for them and say so.
  • Git/CI: Forgejo + Woodpecker; cost: re-learning pipeline idioms; benefit: runners on your mesh with your secrets.
  • Monitoring: Prometheus trio per the free-monitoring guide; cost: one evening; benefit: every other swap becomes observable.

Migration order that respects weekends

Sequence by reversibility and blast radius: analytics and monitoring first (read-only, zero user impact), then notes and passwords (small data, high habit value), then photos (large data, patient users), then chat (network effects -- migrate groups together or not at all), and mail last, after deliverability homework, with the old account forwarding for a full quarter. Each step ends with the portability checklist from the exit guide signed off: open export verified, restore drilled once, runbook page written. The order's logic is emotional as much as technical: early wins build the household's trust budget, and mail -- the swap most likely to frustrate strangers who email you -- spends from that budget, so it goes last, when the budget is rich.

What I still rent, publicly

Which swaps have the worst import tooling?

Mail history (IMAP sync tools vary wildly; budget a test mailbox first) and chat history (export formats are political, not technical). Photos and notes import beautifully almost everywhere, which is why they sit early in the order: your first weekends should taste the good tooling, so the household's morale survives the mail quarter.

Do these swaps survive contact with non-technical family?

The client-first ones do (photos, passwords, notes), because the experience lives in apps they already trust visually. The server-shaped ones (mail, chat federation) survive only with the one-page runbook and a named owner -- non-technical users do not fear self-hosting; they fear unanswered questions at dinner, and the runbook plus owner exists precisely to answer at dinner speed.
