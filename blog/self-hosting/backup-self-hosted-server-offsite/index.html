How Do I Back Up a Self-Hosted Server Offsite Cheaply?

How to back up a self-hosted server offsite cheaply: restic to B2 or a Storage Box, encryption, bandwidth shaping and the restore test that proves it all works.

Most homes land on object storage for the daily chain plus one air-gapped or friend-hosted copy for the catastrophic tier -- two targets, one tool, one runbook. The vendor mix is deliberate: correlated failure (one company hosting both copies) is the risk diversification buys, and the restore-frequency Q&A supplies the drill cadence that makes either copy more than insurance theater.

The setup that stays boring

The include-list choice is a hill I die on: exclusion-glob backups rot silently (someone adds --exclude '*.db' for one incident and your database vanishes from every future snapshot), while an explicit list fails loudly when a new path appears unlisted -- add a weekly check that diffs served paths against the list and alerts on drift. Bandwidth limits belong in the timer, not in your memory; retention policies belong in the runbook with a one-sentence reason per rule, because pruning is deletion and deletion deserves documented intent.

Verification: the part that makes it a backup

An offsite copy you have never restored is a subscription to hope. Wire the weekly automated partial restore (mount, three random paths, canary diff) against the offsite repo at least monthly -- network restores surface the failure class local drills cannot: egress limits, API throttling, and the chunk-size versus latency interaction that makes remote restores crawl. Time every drill; log duration beside size so "restore window" is a measured number in your runbook rather than a prayer. When a quarterly full drill completes, you own the only fact that matters in an incident: hours-to-data, verified, this quarter, by you.

Failure modes worth pre-grieving

  • Token expiry or policy change at the provider: rotation calendar plus a second target already configured (init takes minutes; panicking takes nights).
  • Repository lock stuck after a killed run: restic unlock exists, and your runbook says when it is safe -- after confirming no concurrent job, never blindly.
  • Encryption password loss: the password lives in your escrow envelope alongside the recovery shards from the exit guide's household governance section; test the envelope annually like any other backup.
  • Slow creep: dataset grows past the bandwidth window; the duration log's trend line tells you a month before the timer starts overlapping your morning.

Each row is cheap insurance written once; together they convert the scariest sentence in self-hosting -- "the house burned down" -- into a procurement-and-restore weekend with known hours, which is precisely the difference the worth-it analysis prices when it says automation is what makes ownership survivable.

restic or borg for offsite?

restic for object storage and cross-platform restores (single static binary, S3/B2/Azure/SFTP backends); borg when the target is a Unix box you control and dedupe-plus-compression efficiency dominates (server-side dedupe saves bytes restic's client-side model cannot). Mixing is fine and common: borg locally for speed, restic offsite for portability -- one runbook, two tools, zero religion.

How much bandwidth should I cap?

Cap at roughly half your uplink's sustained rate so interactive traffic keeps priority, then raise only if drills show the window shrinking below your RTO. Measure the capped throughput once per quarter: providers throttle differently over time, and a cap chosen in 2024 may starve a 2026 dataset -- the duration trend line is your only honest witness.

Should backups be immutable against ransomware?

Yes where the target offers it: B2 object lock or a storage box with append-only keys prevents deletion even with stolen credentials, converting ransomware's "your backups too" into "your old backups too, from before the lock window". Pair with one air-gapped or offline-credential copy for the truly catastrophic tier; immutability plus separation plus tested restores is the triad, and any two of three leaves a documented hole.
