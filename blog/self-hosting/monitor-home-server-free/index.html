How Do I Monitor a Home Server With Free Tools Only?

How to monitor a home server with free tools only: node_exporter plus Prometheus plus Grafana, SMART and UPS alerts, and the five dashboards worth building.

The free stack, assembled in an evening

Run all three in containers or compose stacks from the cluster's compose library, on the mesh addresses so monitoring survives internet outages -- the night your WAN dies is precisely the night you want dashboards reachable from the couch. Resource cost is trivial (the trio idles under 300 MB combined at home scale), which matters on boxes sized per the RAM sizing Q&A; keep retention at 15-30 days locally and export daily summaries if you want year-scale trends without year-scale disks.

The five dashboards that earn their place

  • Pressure and load: CPU/IO/memory PSI averages, load versus core count, thermal curves -- the "is it suffering" page, read before any other.
  • Disks: SMART attributes (reallocated, pending sectors, temperature), capacity trends per dataset, scrub and smart-test result gauges.
  • Heartbeats: backup success per repository, certificate days-remaining per name, tunnel liveness per path, update-job exits -- the silence page.
  • Network: mesh peer handshake ages, WAN latency percentiles, DNS query rates and failure ratios.
  • Power: UPS charge, runtime estimate, outlet wattage where smart plugs report -- the page that predicts rather than reports.

Everything else you build in month one gets deleted by month three; these five survive because each answers one recurring household question ("is it hot/slow/dying/unbacked/unreachable/on battery?") without interpretation. Dashboard minimalism is alerting minimalism's precondition: every panel implies a potential page, and pages without owners are noise with credentials.

Alert rules: few, owned, silence-first

Route them where you actually look (ntfy to your phone, a matrix channel, email as fallback) and rehearse each once: unplug the backup timer, watch the page arrive, restore, close. The restore-drill cadence and the cert heartbeat guide supply the two most valuable inputs; the UPS rule pairs with the power-loss Q&A in this cluster, whose shutdown automation feeds on the same exporter. Four alerts feel ascetic until you remember the alternative's shape: thirty rules, twelve ignored, and the one that matters buried in a digest nobody reads.

Beyond the trio, still free

Log aggregation without a vendor: Loki-plus-promtail if you want queryable history, or plain journalctl shipped nightly to the backup target if you want honesty with less RAM -- at home scale, grep over shipped journals answers 90% of questions and survives your monitoring stack's own outage, a resilience property fancier stacks quietly lack. Uptime probes from outside (a friend's box, a cheap VPS, or the federation's status tooling) complete the picture by seeing what your LAN cannot: WAN reachability, tunnel health from the internet's side, and certificate chains as strangers verify them. Free, in this stack, never meant toy: it meant auditable, exportable, and owned -- the three properties the exit guide calls sovereignty, applied to the instruments that watch everything else.

Is pushing metrics from homes behind NAT a problem?

Prometheus pulls, so the server must be reachable from Prometheus -- solved by putting Prometheus on the mesh (it scrapes mesh addresses) rather than exposing exporters inbound. Push-based alternatives (pushgateway) exist but invert failure semantics (silent death looks like stale success); prefer pull-from-mesh and keep the heartbeat alerts that make staleness loud.

How do I monitor the monitor?

Two layers: Prometheus's own absent() meta-rules (scrape-targets-missing alerts fired by a second, tiny Prometheus or by the federation status tool), and the household rule that the monitoring box's UPS and backups join the first cohort -- an unmonitored monitor fails exactly as silently as the absence it was bought to detect, a recursion every on-call engineer recognizes from larger fleets.

Worth adding Windows or phone clients?

Windows: windows_exporter slots into the same dashboards; phones: ntfy/Matrix clients consume alerts but contribute nothing -- accept that gap honestly (mobile telemetry is a vendor product or nothing). The estate boundary you monitor should match the boundary you own; monitoring devices you cannot act on produces anxiety dashboards, and anxiety is the one resource homelabs generate for free already.
