Sum your actual list, multiply the top two items by 1.5 for load spikes, and you have the workload term. The recurring error in homelab sizing is adding idle numbers and wondering why the box swaps during a photo-sync night: load concurrency, not idle footprint, sets the requirement, and family workloads are bursty by nature -- everyone comes home at six.

Decide with free monitoring, then stop upgrading

Upgrade only when /proc/pressure/memory shows sustained some-avg10 above ~10 during normal evenings, or swap-in counters move without a backup job running. Below that, more RAM buys silence, not speed -- and silence is what monitoring already gives you. The money usually works harder in the exit guide's other lines: disks with scrub histories, a UPS that has actually been load-tested, or the hour of automation that removes a weekly chore. For what these numbers look like on watt-frugal hardware, the mini-PC project list in this cluster ships measured idle draws per build once it goes live; until then, the mesh pilot's node spec (8 GB, 25 W, three-jurisdiction quorum member) is a datapoint I trust more than any benchmark table.

Does swapping ruin a home server or save it?

A small zram or low-swappiness disk swap saves it: bursts absorb without OOM-killing the wrong process, and PSI tells you when reliance becomes chronic. The failure mode to avoid is default desktop swappiness on a box running databases -- tune vm.swappiness down (10), keep swap as shock absorber, and alert on swap-in rate rather than swap existence; presence is policy, usage is signal.
