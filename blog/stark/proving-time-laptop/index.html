Where the scaling curve bends

Memory: the real ceiling

Proving holds the trace matrix, composition intermediates, and FRI layer trees simultaneously; RSS grows roughly linearly in cycles times width, and laptops hit RAM walls before core walls. Three relief valves in production order: stream trace generation where the backend supports segment-wise input (cuts peak RSS 30-40% at slight time cost), prove in batches and aggregate claims at the application layer instead of monolithic traces, and schedule million-row jobs to machines with headroom rather than pretending laptops are servers. The stark cluster index expands each valve with the measurements behind its percentage; the short version is that laptop proving is a development and CI affordance, and fleets exist because memory physics says so.

What this means for CI and product shape

With 3-4 second proofs at 65k cycles, proof-carrying CI is comfortable per commit; at minute-scale proofs it becomes a batching decision (prove per merge, not per commit) rather than a removal candidate -- the gate's value does not depreciate with its runtime, only its placement changes. Product-side, the same numbers set UX: interactive proving should cap near 262k cycles with a queued-path beyond, because users forgive a queue and never forgive a frozen tab. These are the conversations where measured laptop numbers earn their keep: capacity planning for cryptography is still capacity planning, and the zkVM tutorial's benchmark section exists to give yours the same manifest-disciplined starting point.

When does proving time justify recursion or batching?

When aggregate verifier-side or storage costs dominate (thousands of small claims: batch them, cutting per-claim prove time up to 40%) or when chains of claims must collapse to one artifact (recursion, above the break-even thresholds measured in the cluster's recursion Q&A). For a lone laptop workload, neither: the honest optimization order is layout, padding awareness, then threads -- recursion is an architecture decision wearing a performance costume.
