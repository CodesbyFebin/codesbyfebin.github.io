How Should I Benchmark a Prover Honestly?

How to benchmark a prover honestly: hardware manifests, warm caches, thread pins, security-bit reporting and the five numbers every credible benchmark table carries.

The manifest: every number's passport

Workloads: canonical, versioned, adversarial

  • Canonical ladder: 2^12, 2^16, 2^20 cycles of one pinned program -- the trend line that catches slope regressions.
  • Shape variants: memory-heavy and compute-heavy programs at equal cycles, because width-versus-length economics differ and single-shape benches hide half the surface.
  • Adversarial cases: padding-boundary workloads (2^16+1 cycles) and degenerate traces (all-idle), where implementations misbehave first and benchmarks rarely look.

Method: interleaving, warmup, and the spread you must show

Run configs interleaved (A/B/A/B) rather than sequentially: thermal drift and background noise then affect both arms equally, and median-of-runs with interquartile spreads survives the laptop on your desk. Warm caches deliberately (one discarded run) and say so; report p50 and p95, never best-of, because best-of is the number least likely to resemble anyone's production. For verify-time claims, include cold-start (first verification after load) beside steady-state: verifiers in serverless shapes pay cold starts, and a table hiding them misprices exactly the deployment that reads it.

Publishing: slopes over points, negatives included

Points date; slopes teach. Publish time-versus-cycles and size-versus-cycles curves with their fits (near-linear, logarithmic), so readers can extrapolate to their workloads instead of interpolating your vibes. Include peak RSS always -- the memory column is where laptop claims die, per the cycle-ceiling Q&A -- and include failures: a regression you caught and reverted is a benchmark result about your process, and processes are what readers actually adopt. The five-number rule (time, size, RSS, verify, manifest) plus slopes plus negatives is the house standard; every prover table in this cluster obeys it, which is why the tables disagree with vendor numbers occasionally and with each other never.
