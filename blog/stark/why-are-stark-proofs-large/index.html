Why Are STARK Proofs Larger Than SNARK Proofs?

Why STARK proofs are larger than SNARK proofs: query counts, grinding, field size and FRI layers — plus when the size stops mattering in real deployments.

Where the bytes actually go

  • Trace commitments: a few 32-byte Merkle roots -- cheap, not the problem.
  • FRI layers: each folding round commits a smaller polynomial; the stack of roots and final constant costs a few hundred bytes.
  • Query openings: the dominant term -- 30-60 queries, each carrying Merkle paths through trace and FRI trees (log-depth × 32 bytes, times several trees).
  • Grinding + manifest: nonce and parameter envelope; small but non-zero, and worth keeping for auditability.

Do the arithmetic at 40 queries and 20-deep trees and you land exactly where measurements land: 60-200 KB for typical zkVM proofs, versus ~200 bytes for Groth16 or 1-3 KB for recursive-friendly SNARKs. The difference is structural, not immaturity: hash-based sampling proves proximity by showing many authenticated spots, while pairings let SNARKs check a global algebraic identity in one shot. You are paying in bytes for not paying in trust.

SNARK proofs would not grow at all -- their advantage is constant size -- but their prover cost and setup story scale differently, which is the trade the comparison table prices across verifier environments.

When size matters, and when it doesn't

Honest mitigations (and one dishonest one)

Legitimate size reducers: raise field efficiency only where security allows (never the reverse), tune queries to the actual security target with published analysis, batch multiple claims into one proof (amortization is real and large), and recurse when aggregation thresholds are met -- the break-even measurements live in the cluster's recursion Q&A once live. The dishonest mitigation is weakening grinding or query counts to shrink proofs: it buys kilobytes with soundness bits, and because parameters travel in the manifest, every honest verifier rejects the result anyway -- you will have shipped a larger failure, not a smaller proof. For the layout-side economics that dominate prover time rather than proof size, see the trace layout article; both articles share one thesis: measure the component before optimizing the adjective.

Will STARK proofs get smaller over time?

Incrementally, yes: better folding schedules, base-field FRI variants, and aggregation research shave tens of percent across years. Order-of-magnitude shrinkage would change the proof system's trust or security story, so expect convergence toward "small enough for off-chain everywhere, wrapped for on-chain", not parity with pairing-based bytes.

Does compression help (gzip the proof)?

Barely: proof bytes are hashes and field elements, already high-entropy; gzip typically saves under 5%. If a channel demands smaller, the honest tools are batching and recursion -- or storing the proof once content-addressed and sending the 32-byte hash plus a retrieval hint, which is what PRF-1 relays do by design.

Is 100 KB acceptable for mobile clients?

Usually yes on modern networks (one image tile costs more), and verification's millisecond CPU cost matters more for battery than the transfer. For constrained IoT, the pattern is delegate: a gateway verifies and returns a signed attestation, or the device runs a minimal verifier like tiny-verify over a locally cached proof -- both keep mobile cost near zero without shrinking the proof itself.
