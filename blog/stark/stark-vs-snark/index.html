The decision table

Measure this comparison on your own workload. No timing or memory table from the draft is presented as an observed result.

Read the table by column of consequence, not by row: if your verifier is a smart contract paying gas per byte, proof size dominates and SNARKs win despite setup pain. If your verifier is a server, browser, or CI job, verification milliseconds are free and STARK transparency plus prover speed dominate. The middle column -- modern universal-setup SNARKs -- is where recursion ecosystems live, and where most "STARK vs SNARK" debates actually resolve into "which recursion stack".

Trust models: ceremonies versus hash functions

A Groth16 setup concentrates existential risk: whoever holds the toxic waste can forge proofs forever, so communities run multiparty ceremonies with public audits -- admirable, complex, and forever tied to the ceremony's integrity story. STARKs replace that story with "SHA-2 is preimage-resistant", a claim auditors can test and decades-old hardware can verify. For sovereignty-focused infrastructure -- archives, federations, compliance artifacts meant to outlive their builders -- setup-free soundness is not a technical preference but an archival property: a proof whose trust depends on a 2019 ceremony video is a proof with a expiry date nobody signed.

Choosing in one paragraph

On-chain verification or byte-budgeted verifiers: SNARK, preferring universal-setup families unless gas per byte forces Groth16. Off-chain verification, long computations, transparency requirements, or archival lifetimes: STARK, as implemented in the zkVM tutorial and rust-stark-zkvm. Recursion-heavy roadmaps: evaluate both, because aggregation changes the economics -- the cluster's recursion Q&A (in production next) documents where STARK-of-STARKs currently breaks even. And when a vendor answers "which proof system?" with a brand name before asking your verifier environment and artifact lifetime, you have learned something useful about the vendor.

Hybrid patterns: composition as insurance

The operational lesson is mundane but decisive: manage the envelopes as one lifecycle. A wrapper whose inner proof rotated unnoticed is a lie with valid syntax, so pinning, manifests, and rotation schedules must span the composition, not its parts. A third pattern is temporal: launch with the system your verifier demands today while archiving the artifacts tomorrow's migration needs -- traces or re-provable inputs, parameter documentation, hashes of everything. Proof-system portability is not a technical feature, it is a record-keeping discipline. Teams that treat proof-system choice as a marriage discover divorce costs; teams that treat it as a dated, documented decision migrate twice without incident. The table above chooses your starting system; this section chooses your exits -- and in sovereignty engineering, the exits are half the design.
