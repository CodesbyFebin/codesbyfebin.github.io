Baselines first: the discipline that makes flags science

Before touching Cargo.toml, freeze two numbers: a criterion suite covering your hot paths (with saved baselines and CI regression alerts) and one end-to-end workload replay with wall-clock and RSS logged. Everything after is a diff against these. Teams that skip baselines report "release got faster" while their p99 quietly regressed -- aggregate speed and tail behavior diverge regularly under LTO, and only per-bench deltas show which half you bought. The slow-release Q&A catalogs the failure modes this discipline catches; this article is the constructive path through the same territory.

PGO and BOLT: the measured-optional tier

Per-crate overrides and dependency hygiene

Global profiles are blunt; cargo's per-package overrides are the scalpel: hot dependencies (crypto, serialization) at opt-level = 3 while your workspace stays at 2; build scripts and proc macros at 0 because they run once at compile time and their "optimization" only slows your builds. Dev-profile dependency optimization ([profile.dev.package."*"] opt-level = 1) keeps test suites fast without touching release semantics. Audit overrides quarterly against baselines -- dependency updates shift inlining landscapes, and an override that earned its place in 2025 may be pure compile tax in 2026, a drift class the CI baseline alerts exist to catch before humans argue about it.

Shipping: artifacts, symbols, and the paper trail

Release engineering closes the loop: stripped artifacts to users, unstripped twins with build-id into your symbol store (post-hoc flamegraphs from field crashes depend on it), profile flags and benchmark deltas recorded in the release note's footer, and the binary fingerprint (profile, git hash, toolchain) printed at --version so any field report names its own build. That footer discipline turns "it got slower after the update" into a two-minute baseline diff instead of an excavation -- the same manifest philosophy the proof cluster applies to cryptographic parameters, because shipped configuration without provenance is just rumor with a version number. When a field report arrives, the fingerprint answers which baseline its binary predates, and the benchmark footer answers whether the reported slowness is regression or expectation -- two questions that otherwise consume an afternoon each, every release, forever. Optimizations without that paper trail are indistinguishable from vandalism six months later; the trail is what turns a Cargo.toml from folklore into a dated, argued, reversible record. For size-constrained targets the companion binary-size Q&A continues the ledger into kilobytes; for builds that feel slow rather than binaries that run slow, the slow-release Q&A remains the diagnostic twin of this constructive guide.

How do I keep PGO profiles from going stale?

Regenerate in CI on a schedule (weekly) from the canonical replay, and alert when the PGO build's benchmark delta versus non-PGO shrinks below half its historical median -- staleness shows up as vanishing advantage long before users feel it. Pin the replay fixture in-repo beside the profile job so "the workload" is versioned code, not someone's laptop script.
