WebRTC data channels and the trust boundary of peer-to-peer play

A practical design guide to signaling, mesh growth, peer authority, privacy, connection failures, and lifecycle cleanup.

Separate signaling from gameplay

WebRTC peers exchange connection information through signaling, then establish a data channel under the network conditions available to them. The supplied template describes a mesh primitive and a small signaling route to implement. A signaling endpoint does not itself provide authoritative game-state validation.

Count the connections

In a full mesh, each participant connects to every other participant. The number of peer pairs is n times n minus one divided by two: four participants need six pairs, while eight need twenty-eight. Room size affects connection management and upload demand. Measure the actual payload rate before choosing a capacity limit.

Choose the authority model

A peer can modify its client and send false positions or scores. Cooperative play among known participants has different requirements from competitive ranking. If the product requires trusted results, define an authoritative validation path rather than accepting peer messages as proof of fair play.

Make connection failures visible

NAT and firewall behavior can prevent direct connectivity. Applications may require relay infrastructure depending on their design. The supplied template's failure state should be surfaced per peer; do not leave a participant in a permanent connecting state. This article makes no measured latency or connection-success claim.

Handle lifecycle and privacy

Leaving a room should close data channels, release listeners, and stop retry activity. Reconnect behavior needs stable participant identity and a clear rule for stale messages. Peer connectivity can expose network information to participants, so explain the product's privacy boundary before matchmaking with strangers.

Test a small room

Connect two test clients, send ordered state updates, disconnect one participant, and inspect recovery. Repeat with a third participant and verify that a departing peer does not leave ghost state. Introduce a forged score message to confirm that the chosen authority model handles it as intended. These are proposed checks, not reported results.
