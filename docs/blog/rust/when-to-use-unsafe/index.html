When to Use unsafe in Rust: A Reviewer's Checklist

When to use unsafe in Rust: FFI, raw pointers and layout tricks — with a reviewer's checklist, SAFETY comment standards and miri tests that keep you honest.

The three legitimate territories

  • FFI: calling C (or being called): pointers cross as untrusted input, convert to owned Rust types at the border, and never leak *mut inward. The 4 KB C verifier in tiny-verify is this territory in pure form.
  • Hardware and memory-mapped state: volatile reads/writes to device registers, DMA buffers the compiler must not reorder -- embedded Rust's daily reality per the embedded guide, where volatile is the vocabulary of "the hardware writes behind your back".
  • Proven data structures: intrusive lists, arenas with handle indices, lock-free queues -- structures whose correctness argument is global ("handles index only this arena") and therefore invisible to the borrow checker's local reasoning.

Notice what is absent: "for speed" as a standalone justification. Measured needs do qualify -- but the measurement precedes the unsafe, not follows it, and the benchmark ships with the SAFETY comment, because an unsafe block without its benchmark is a debt with no invoice.

The review checklist, applied without mercy

Miri and tests: the machines that review with you

Miri interprets MIR with full UB detection: uninitialized reads, aliasing violations, out-of-bounds -- the exact classes unsafe introduces. Wire it to run the unsafe-path test subset in CI (slower, decisive), and feed it adversarial inputs: truncated buffers, misaligned casts, double-frees attempted through your safe wrappers (they must be impossible by construction -- a wrapper test that "succeeds" at misuse is a wrapper bug). Beyond miri, property-test the safe surface: thousands of random operation sequences against the structure, comparing to a naive safe model -- the differential-execution trick from the proof cluster, transferred: two implementations, one oracle, every divergence a bug named before shipping.

Culture: unsafe as licensed activity, not forbidden fruit

Banning unsafe outright produces worse outcomes than licensing it: hidden unsafe in dependencies you do not audit, or abandoned performance work that forks into C with zero review. The licensed model -- checklist, miri, size limits, named maintainers, design notes -- makes each block a reviewed artifact with an owner and an invoice. New contributors earn unsafe rights the way they earn merge rights: by reviewing others' first, which is also how the checklist stays alive rather than fossilized. The cultural property that matters is not absence but accountability: in a codebase where every unsafe line names its invariant and its maintainer, "who promised this pointer was valid?" has an answer, and an answer is the difference between an engineering discipline and a séance -- the same transparency argument this portfolio makes about proofs, federations, and budgets, applied at the scale of a single keyword.
