Design questions that pick the rung for you

  • Who writes? One writer, many readers: channel or watch channel (latest-value semantics) -- readers tolerate the newest value, nobody locks.
  • Can readers tolerate staleness? Yes: publish snapshots behind an ArcSwap-style swap or a RwLock read path; no: you need transactional consistency, accept the Mutex.
  • Is the state a fact or a process? Facts (counts, flags) are atomics; processes (a pool, a cache with eviction policy) are owner tasks -- modeling a process as a lock invites its policy logic into every caller.

When review debates Mutex-versus-RwLock for more than five minutes, the real question is usually rung selection: draw who owns what, and the lock debate evaporates into a channel diagram. That drawing habit is the transferable skill; the systems guide's ownership section is its longer form, and the Rc-vs-Arc Q&A covers the pointer-type question that arrives immediately after.

What about sharing a database pool?

The pool is itself the owner-task pattern industrialized: it holds connections, hands out guarded leases via an internal queue, and enforces limits. Share the pool handle (Arc inside the library) freely; never share raw connections across tasks. Configure pool size from measured concurrency, alert on checkout wait p99 -- a starving pool presents as mysterious handler latency, and the wait metric is the only honest witness.
