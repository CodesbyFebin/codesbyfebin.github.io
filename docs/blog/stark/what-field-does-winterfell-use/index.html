Changing fields: what actually moves

Base field versus extension field, briefly

Winterfell separates the base field (trace elements) from the extension field (auxiliary columns, DEEP points, FRI folding challenges). You choose the base; extension degrees follow security analysis. Practical consequence: auxiliary columns cost more per column than base columns (extension elements are wider), which is why the AIR article budgets them sparingly -- permutation receipts yes, speculative intermediates no. When reading third-party parameter sets, check both field ids: a manifest naming only "the field" is hiding half the geometry your verifier needs to assert.

Manifest discipline: fields as deployed configuration

Is the Goldilocks field "post-quantum safe"?

Field choice is not where quantum risk lives -- Shor attacks pairings and discrete logs, not prime-field arithmetic per se; the quantum-relevant choices are hash-based commitments (safe) versus pairing-based systems (not) and security-bit margins against Grover halving. A 64-bit-field STARK at 96-bit parameters keeps ~48+ bits against quantum adversaries: fine for decade-scale artifacts, thin for century-scale ones, which is precisely the lifetime-based guidance the comparison article gives for choosing bits.
