Escape hatches when you outgrow the box

  • Batching first: aggregate independent claims per trace until you approach, not exceed, the boundary; per-claim prove time drops up to 40% and RSS per claim drops further.
  • Segmented proving: where the backend supports stream-wise trace input, trade a few percent time for 30-40% peak RSS -- the single best lever for staying on small machines.
  • Queue to big metal: million-cycle jobs belong on machines with headroom; a proof queue with SLAs is infrastructure, not defeat, and the gateway pattern from the AI cluster ports directly (quotas, journals, idempotent jobs).

Monitoring the ceiling before it monitors you

Four metrics make cycle economics boring: cycles per job class (trend lines expose workload creep), padding ratio (the cliff's early warning), peak RSS per proof (the true capacity signal; CPU utilization lies), and queue age at the big-metal tier (the escape hatch's health). Alert on trend, not threshold: a 10% month-over-month cycle growth in a flagship job class is a design conversation today and an outage story next quarter. This is ordinary capacity planning wearing cryptographic clothing, and the teams that treat it that way -- charts, owners, forecasts -- are exactly the teams whose proving fleets age quietly, per the operations philosophy in systems operations.
